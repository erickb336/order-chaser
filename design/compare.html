Live chase screen: 3 directions

The live chase screen is the core screen: you watch it while the tool moves your order. Each column below shows the same chase in the same state, in a different shape. Pick a state to compare how each shape tells you what is happening.

Prototype: chase state
A

Price ladder

The order book as rows of prices. Your order and the cap sit in the book.

Strength: shows exactly where your order sits in the market, tick by tick.

Weakness: you must read a book to learn the one thing you care about: is it filling, and what does it cost.

B Recommended

Status card

One sentence of state, one number (filled), one price rail.

Strength: answers "what is happening, how much filled, what next" in one glance, in plain words.

Weakness: hides the depth of the book; a trader who wants to see the queue gets less.

C

Timeline

Every action of the tool as a dated event, newest at the bottom.

Strength: full audit trail; you can check that the tool did each safe step in order.

Weakness: the present state is the last line of a list; filled amount and prices are hard to see.

Recommendation: B, the status card, with the timeline below it

You start a chase to get a good fill with no effort. While it runs, you need three answers: what the tool does now, how much filled, and the worst it can cost. B gives all three at the top, in words. The price rail still shows your order against the best bid, the best ask and the cap.

From C, the full flow keeps the event log, under the card. It is the proof that the tool cancelled, confirmed and re-read before each fallback. From A, it keeps nothing: the book depth is noise for one small order at the top of the book.

Rejected shapes: a candlestick chart (shows price history, not your order); a pure number dashboard of 8 tiles (no single main point); a full-screen ladder with click-to-trade (that is a trading terminal, not a chaser).
